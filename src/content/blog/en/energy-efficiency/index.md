Rising energy prices have turned energy efficiency from a niche topic into one of the first questions buyers ask when viewing a new property. In this article, we break down what's actually worth paying attention to, and how these elements affect the cost of running a home over the years.

## Insulation — the foundation, not an extra

Before talking about heat pumps or solar panels, it's worth understanding that none of these solutions work optimally without good building insulation. Walls, roofs, and windows with poor insulation values mean that even the best heat pump will run inefficiently, using more energy than it should.

When evaluating a property, it's worth asking the developer for specific insulation values (U-factor) rather than just the building's general energy rating.

## Heat pumps as the standard

Heat pumps have become the primary heating source in new construction in recent years — and not by accident. Compared to traditional boilers, a heat pump can significantly reduce heating costs, especially when combined with good insulation and a solar installation.

![An outdoor heat pump unit mounted on a building's exterior wall](./pompa-ciepla.jpg)

It's worth checking the pump's own efficiency rating (COP coefficient) and whether the installation was properly sized for the building's floor area and characteristics — a poorly matched heat pump can end up costing more than expected.

## Solar panels — an investment that pays for itself

A solar installation, particularly paired with a heat pump, can significantly cut energy bills, and in many cases nearly eliminate them during sunnier months. It's also a real selling point when it comes to resale — buyers increasingly see solar panels as a standard feature rather than a luxury extra.

## What this means for property value

Homes with a high energy rating:

- generate lower monthly running costs, which directly affects buyers' borrowing capacity,
- are easier to rent out — tenants increasingly ask about heating bills during the viewing itself,
- retain higher market value over time, as regulatory requirements around building energy efficiency continue to tighten.
